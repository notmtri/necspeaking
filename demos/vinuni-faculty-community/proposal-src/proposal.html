1Executive summary

International faculty make some of the biggest decisions about their move to Hanoi, such as where to live, which school to choose and how to insure their family, before they arrive and before they have anyone to ask. Partners and children face the move too, but have no place in university systems. This proposal asks the council to approve a one-semester pilot of a small, private community platform that closes those gaps.

What the platform does

  • Starts before arrival. New hires get a personal invitation link as soon as they sign their contract, with an arrival plan timed to their start date.
  • Includes families. Partners get a limited guest account linked to the faculty member, so the person visiting schools and viewing apartments can take part.
  • Connects people. A privacy-aware directory and a buddy programme pair every newcomer with an experienced colleague, with a coordinator confirming each match.
  • Keeps answers findable. One Ask & Discuss space, curated Living in Hanoi guides and dated recommendations replace scattered emails and chat threads. A weekly digest keeps the existing WhatsApp and Zalo groups in the loop instead of competing with them.
  • Is accountable. Consent is recorded for every optional personal field, official information is kept separate from peer advice, and the pilot is judged against four outcome targets.

Where this comes from

The platform builds directly on the International Faculty Community Platform proposal by Marc Kramer, Senior Lecturer (September 2026). It keeps that proposal’s directory, guides, events, recommendations and buddy matching, and its Microsoft 365 direction. It adds eight refinements, summarised in Section 3: mainly earlier access, family inclusion, a lighter start, and measurable outcomes.

2The problem

Relocating to a new country is a family project with a hard deadline. Today, the support around it is fragmented: official information sits in HR documents and emails, practical knowledge lives in colleagues’ heads and in fast-moving chat groups, and nothing connects a newcomer to the right person at the right time.

Five gaps

#GapWhat it looks like in practice
1Help arrives too lateHousing, school places and insurance for dependants are usually decided weeks before arrival, but a university account, and with it most internal channels, only starts on the contract start date.
2Families are invisiblePartners often handle school visits, house hunting and daily logistics, and are the most isolated members of a relocating family, yet they have no access to university information or community.
3Knowledge is scattered and decaysGood answers are given in chat groups and private messages, then scroll away. Advice about realtors, clinics and banks goes out of date with no way to tell.
4Newcomers don’t know whom to askWithout a way to find colleagues by expertise, language, family stage or interest, the same few helpful people receive every question.
5No one can tell if support is workingThere is no shared measure of whether new faculty feel settled, whether questions get answered, or whether a buddy relationship actually happened.

What a good outcome looks like

The original proposal states the test well: “A new colleague should be able to find people with shared interests, identify someone who can answer a relocation question, discover trusted community information, and receive a personal connection without needing to know whom to ask.” This proposal adds two conditions: that this should be true before the colleague arrives, and for their family as well.

Why a platform, and why now

The university already recruits internationally every semester, and each cohort rebuilds the same knowledge from scratch. A small, well-run platform turns that effort into something that accumulates. Starting with a one-semester pilot keeps the cost and risk low, and the results will show whether to go further.

3Building on the original proposal

Marc Kramer’s September 2026 proposal set out a secure, internal community for international faculty: a searchable directory with privacy controls, curated relocation guides, topic forums, events, dated recommendations, a rules-based buddy-matching system, administration and governance, all on Microsoft 365. This proposal keeps that foundation.

What stays the same

  • Faculty profiles in which every optional personal field is opt-in, with family information private by default.
  • Transparent, rules-based matching (no AI) that spreads the load across buddies, with check-ins after a few weeks.
  • A clear separation between official university information and peer advice, with review dates on anything that goes stale.
  • Microsoft 365 as the technology base, single sign-on for faculty, role-based permissions and an audit trail.
  • A phased approach: start simple, focus on adoption, then expand.

5Features

This section walks through every feature of the platform, with what it does, why it matters and a screenshot from the prototype. Each feature is marked Pilot if it is proposed for the first semester or Later if it would follow a successful pilot. The prototype shows the full vision so the council can see where the pilot leads. The online demo focuses on the faculty features in 5.1–5.13.

5.1A home page built on three questions Pilot

Every user lands on a page that answers the three questions from the original proposal: Who can I connect with? What information do I need? What is happening? Content is personalised: a newcomer sees suggested buddies and guides for the topics they asked for help with; an established colleague sees new arrivals to welcome and unanswered questions in their areas.

Why it matters. Newcomers shouldn’t have to know the system to benefit from it. The home page brings the right people and information to them, and gives experienced colleagues a low-effort way to help.

5.2Pre-arrival access and the arrival plan Pilot Refinement 1

When HR records a signed contract, the new hire receives a personal invitation link at their personal email address. They can complete a profile, request a buddy, read guides and ask questions straight away. On their start date the university account takes over automatically and everything carries over.

The arrival plan is a checklist timed to the arrival date: choose a neighbourhood eight weeks out, shortlist schools at seven, meet the buddy on video at six, sort out dependants’ insurance at five, and so on through the arrival week. Each step links to the relevant guide or conversation, and the plan is shared with the partner.

Key capabilities

  • Invitation sent automatically from HR’s contract record
  • Guest access until the start date, then single sign-on
  • Nine timed steps, each linked to a guide
  • Shared and editable by the faculty member and partner
  • The buddy step completes itself when a match is confirmed
Why it matters. Support that starts on day one is already too late for housing, schools and insurance. This moves help to when decisions are actually made.

5.3Guest access for partners and families Pilot Refinement 2

A faculty member can add their partner as a guest. Guests can read and post in Ask & Discuss (their posts are clearly marked Guest), use the guides, RSVP to events marked “partners and families welcome”, and share the arrival plan. They cannot see the faculty directory or anyone’s contact details. A Partners & families topic gives them a space of their own, for example to organise a beginners’ Vietnamese class. Access ends automatically when the linked faculty member leaves or removes them.

Why it matters. A move that works for the faculty member but not for their family often doesn’t last. Including partners costs little and protects faculty privacy by design.

5.4Profiles with privacy controls Pilot Refinement 7

Profiles follow the field list in the original proposal: name, college, expertise and university email are required, and everything else is optional. Each optional field has its own visibility: visible to faculty, matching only (used to suggest buddies but never displayed), or only me. Family information is private by default and uses children’s age ranges, never names or birth dates. Home addresses are never collected, only a broad area such as “Ocean Park” for matching.

Key capabilities

  • Required, recommended and optional fields clearly labelled
  • Per-field visibility; consent recorded with a date and withdrawable at any time
  • Preview as others see it shows exactly what colleagues will see
  • Download my data and delete optional data in one click
  • Completeness meter, and a reminder to review the profile every 12 months (adjustable)
  • Volunteer as a buddy and set a personal cap on how many

5.5Faculty directory Pilot

Faculty can search by name, expertise or interest and filter by college, help topic (“can help with schools”), hobby, language, buddy availability, and, only for people who chose to share it, whether they have children. A profile panel shows what the person has made visible, with one-click contact through their preferred channel.

5.6Buddy programme Pilot Refinement 5

A newcomer says what matters to them by marking six factors as Off, Nice to have or Key: college, country or language familiarity, family stage (private), hobbies, help topics and broad area. The platform suggests three to five buddies with a transparent score: anyone can open “Why this score?” to see the breakdown. Suggestions are adjusted for workload, so a buddy who is at their cap stops appearing and the same helpful people don’t get every newcomer.

In the pilot, a coordinator confirms every match using the suggestions and the newcomer’s request. Once confirmed, both people receive an introduction. Three weeks later the newcomer gets a private check-in (“Going well”, “We haven’t connected yet”, or “I’d like a different match”), and a re-match is one click away.

5.7Ask & Discuss Pilot Refinement 3

All questions and conversations live in one stream, organised by topic tags (schools, housing, health and insurance, food, sports, banking, weekend, book club, new to Hanoi, partners and families). When someone types a question, the platform suggests the right topic and shows how many colleagues have offered help on it. Those colleagues are notified, instead of the whole faculty being emailed. Unanswered questions are easy to filter, and moderators are alerted after 48 hours.

Sensitive topics carry standing disclaimers: housing posts are not university endorsements of any realtor; health posts link to the official HR benefits guide; banking posts are not financial advice. Posts can be marked helpful or reported, and moderators can pin key threads.

5.8Working with the WhatsApp and Zalo groups Pilot Refinement 4

Most day-to-day chat among faculty already happens in WhatsApp and Zalo groups. Rather than asking people to leave them, the platform posts a weekly digest there (new answers, upcoming events and unanswered questions), and moderators can save a good answer from a chat into Ask & Discuss with the author’s permission, so it can be searched later. New hires can ask for a group invite from their arrival plan.

5.9Living in Hanoi guides Pilot

Curated guides cover the first week, choosing where to live, schools, banking, health insurance and dependants, medical care, getting around, and international groceries. Each guide is labelled Official (maintained by HR or the International Office) or Faculty-contributed, names an owner, and shows when it was last reviewed. Overdue guides are flagged. Readers can report outdated information, and each guide links to related conversations.

5.10Faculty recommendations Pilot

Colleagues recommend realtors, clinics, schools, shops and activities. Every recommendation carries the date it was last confirmed and how many people agree. A colour shows how fresh it is: current (confirmed within 6 months), review due (6–12 months) or likely outdated (over a year). One click confirms it is still accurate. Businesses carry a standard note that the recommendation is not a VinUniversity endorsement.

5.11Events and activities Pilot

A shared calendar and list cover social, cultural, sports, family and weekend activities, organised by faculty or by the university (official events are highlighted). Anyone can create an event. RSVPs show capacity and fill a waitlist when full, and events suitable for families are marked as welcoming partners. Interested people are notified, and a reminder is sent the day before. Calendar integration with Outlook is Later.

5.12Search, notifications and feedback Pilot

One search box finds people, conversations, guides and events. Notifications cover replies, buddy introductions, check-ins and reminders, with automated ones clearly marked. A Feedback button on every page lets anyone suggest a new topic or report outdated information directly to the administrators.

5.13Works on phones Pilot

Every page adapts to a phone screen. That matters most before arrival, when new hires and partners are likely to read on a phone between packing and paperwork. The navigation collapses into a menu, and tables and calendars reflow.

Note. In the Microsoft 365 build, the same screens would run inside the Teams mobile app and the Power Apps mobile app.

6Administration and governance

A community platform succeeds or fails on upkeep. The administration area gives a small team (one coordinator and a few volunteer moderators) what they need in one place, and makes no change without a trace.

6.1Pilot measures and dashboard Pilot Refinement 8

The coordinator’s first screen shows the four pilot outcome measures against their targets, followed by the operational figures from the original proposal: new users, incomplete profiles, unanswered questions, active topics, upcoming events and pending match requests. Charts show weekly activity and which topics are busiest, and a list of incomplete profiles has a one-click reminder.

6.2Moderation and content review Pilot

Reported posts come with the reason and an excerpt, and are kept or removed with one click. Unanswered questions can be sent to the colleagues who offered help on that topic. Feedback from the Feedback button lands here too. A separate content review list orders every guide and recommendation by how overdue it is, with “Mark reviewed” and “Archive” actions, because housing, banking, school and insurance information goes stale fastest.

6.3Topics and settings, configured without code Pilot

As the original proposal recommends, new topics, interest tags, matching weights and the profile-reminder interval are settings, not programming work. Each topic shows its activity over the last 30 days. When one passes a threshold (eight posts a month in the prototype), it is flagged as ready for its own space with a moderator.

6.4Users and access Pilot

The coordinator sends pre-arrival invitations, sees which partners hold guest accounts, deactivates leavers flagged by the HR sync on their last day, and assigns roles. Every faculty account shows its role, profile completeness and status.

6.5Automations and audit log Pilot

Routine work runs on Power Automate flows that can be switched on and off individually: onboarding, profile reminders, match introductions and check-ins, unanswered-question alerts, event reminders, review reminders, leaver deactivation, pre-arrival invitations and consent-expiry checks. Administrative and moderation actions are recorded in an audit log with who, what and when.

7Privacy, security and compliance

The platform holds personal and, optionally, family information about colleagues. Privacy is therefore part of the design, not an add-on, and the whole design should be reviewed by the university’s Legal / Data Protection function before launch.

Principles

  • Collect the minimum. Only name, college, expertise and university email are required. No home addresses, no children’s names or birth dates, and no marital status.
  • Opt in, field by field. Every optional field has its own visibility and, where personal, its own recorded consent with a stated purpose.
  • Private by default. Family information and phone numbers start hidden. “Matching only” data is used to suggest buddies but never displayed.
  • Internal only. No public access. Faculty sign in with university single sign-on; guests use invitation-based guest accounts with narrower permissions.
  • Least privilege. Technical administrators cannot read private profile fields. Administrative and moderation actions are audited.
  • People stay in control. Anyone can preview their profile as others see it, download their data, withdraw consent or delete all optional data.
  • Official vs peer. Official guidance is labelled and owned by HR or the International Office. Peer advice carries disclaimers, and businesses are never endorsed.

Legal basis: to be confirmed

The design aims to meet the consent and data-subject-rights requirements of Vietnam’s personal data protection framework, including the Law on Personal Data Protection (in force from 1 January 2026) and Decree 13/2023/NĐ-CP. Whether the design is sufficient, and what retention periods apply, must be confirmed by the university’s legal and data-protection review. This proposal does not replace that review.

9Pilot plan

One semester, one cohort, four measures and a decision at the end. The pilot is deliberately small, so it can be run well and judged fairly.

Scope

  • Who: international faculty joining in the Spring 2027 semester and their partners, plus established international faculty as buddies, moderators and contributors.
  • What: the features marked Pilot in Section 5 and the administration in Section 6.
  • Not in the pilot: calendar integration, full Dataverse, Power BI analytics, automated chat-group posting, AI features, buy/sell/give-away.

Two ways in

“I’m new to VinUni”

A four-step onboarding: name and college; title, expertise, home country and languages; arrival date, family and where they will live (optional); and what they would like help with. The dashboard is then built from those answers, with an arrival checklist, buddy suggestions, guides on their topics and upcoming events.

“I’ve been here a while”

A demo sign-in that never asks for a password, straight into a sample account of a faculty member who has been here three years. It shows a buddy request from a newcomer to accept, a current buddy, questions in their areas and new colleagues to welcome.

Suggested things to try

  1. Choose I’m new to VinUni, complete the four steps, and look at the dashboard built for you.
  2. Open My buddy, ask one of the suggested colleagues, then click the button to see what happens when they say yes.
  3. Ask a question in Ask & Discuss and see which colleagues it would be sent to.
  4. Click Start over, choose I’ve been here a while, sign in, and accept the buddy request.
  5. Fill in the Microsoft Form: what you would use, what is missing, and what you would remove or change.

What testers enter stays in their own browser and is not sent anywhere. Start over clears it. The partner and coordinator views described in this document are part of the prototype but not of the online demo.
